All Is Lost
July 26, 2026
Defeat
All is lost. A sentiment rarely uttered, but commonly felt.

One can see it in the eyes of people from all walks of life.

One would expect the feeling is acute terror. But terror has a remarkably short time constant: doom becomes dread, dread becomes apathy and persistent apathy can lead to mania.

In my experience, failure never arises from the acute terror, it is triggered by apathy. The sound of man's defeat is not a gasp, but a whimper.
Purpose
The positive side of this is, you never lose if you never give up; however this also means to lose one must surrender.

And frankly, there's nothing more depressing than seeing a person who has given up completely. Many sad souls spend their whole lives quitting; never persevering; surrendering to apathy.

If I've learned one thing in life, the quitters never end up happy. From the poor Irish farmer to the Silicon Valley billionaire; from the high school dropout to the Quantum physics PhD; I've met them all and the one throughline I can draw is that the "successful" quitter is chronically depressed and the perservant man becomes content.

A chronic quitter may attain success through luck, but they will never be satisfied, they will always yearn for something better in life, that they will never find; because purpose is intrinsic, you cannot develop that purpose if you cannot persevere.

Purpose is crafted on the final rep, the painful last push, the stressful all-nighter, the frightening big leap. This is obvious to the perseverant, but lost on the quitter. Like an addict they are strapped to the hedonic treadmill, yearning for the comfort of safety and mediocrity.
All is Lost Syndrome
The prior content is background knowledge for what I term "All is Lost" syndrome. When ones loses conviction defeat is an inevitability.

Jonestown
In 1978, U.S Congressman Leo Ryan went to visit the Peoples Temple in Jonestown, Guyana. The senator was on a mission to determine if the people of this settlement were being held hostage, there was significant critical media coverage of the settlement which caused the leaders of the Peoples Temple to plan the visit meticulously.

The situation was particularly tense as Ryan had brought relatives of several residents, many of whom believed their family members were being held against their will.

However, after interviewing the residents, Ryan came away surprisingly impressed. Ryan conveyed to the leaders of Jonestown that he thought the place was beautiful and though some wanted to leave, that his report back to the U.S would be broadly positive. For a cult under federal scrutinty, Ryan's approval was a resounding success.

That night, the paramount leader, Jim Jones proclaimed "I have failed... all is lost", he ordered the assassination of the senator and the murder-suicide of all 900 residents of Jonestown including 300 children.

Jones had so adamantly predicted defeat, the concept of success had become an impossibility. The terror of the visit and the chaos of the town had numbed all hope to apathy, and in that apathy the success is meaningless and in fact must be defeated.
Deterministic Defeat
Once one has lost conviction, defeat is guaranteed.
The 2 possible outcomes become:

Failure. Failure confirms the brain's model of the world. The feeling of apathy becomes justified to the self and any effort feels pointless. This creates a self-fulfilling prophecy where a person quits sooner, fails faster and eventually loses their sense of self.

Success. Success is the more dangerous outcome. When a person somehow succeeds or survives despite the mental act of quitting and the sense of apathy, rather than being fulfilled by victory a very odd thing happens. The brain's world model has been violated, defeat has so readily been predicted that they act to destroy their own success.

A form of self-destructive mania takes over. Rash, unjustifiable decisions are made because they just want it all to be over, they've mentally prepared themselves to move on and remaining in the situation creates an acute stress.

This behaviour seems insane, but it is quite common. At the end of a failing startup, you often see manic inexplicable behaviour. Few companies go quietly into the night. When you look at the end of relationships, it's rarely a quiet goodbye, there's the inevitable emotional explosion.
Lesson
The lesson I have drawn from my own life is simple: never give up. When you quit once, it's a lesson, when you quit twice you've accrued a habit.
